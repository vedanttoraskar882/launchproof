Methodology & Workflow

How a LaunchProof validation works

A disciplined five-stage validation process. Timings reflect indicative pilot targets, subject to ongoing platform development and testing.

Step-By-Step Process

Five planned validation stages

Structured progression from initial idea scoping to an evidence-backed commercial decision.

Days 0–2 Indicative Target

1. Define the product idea and investigate demand

Capture the audience, budget and risk appetite. Identify assumptions, research demand trends and review recurring customer complaints.

Days 2–4 Indicative Target

2. Check product economics, pricing and supplier terms

Screen category requirements, compare competitor prices and supplier terms, and calculate landed costs before live testing. Advance to a live test only if there is a viable price range.

Days 4–5 Indicative Target

3. Approve the concept and agree decision thresholds

The founder approves the specification and price range. Check proposed claims, prepare test pages and agree the Go, Revise and Stop thresholds before advertising spend begins.

Days 5–12 Indicative Target

4. Run transparent demand tests at a stated price

Explain to visitors that the product is in development. Compare price-visible waitlist responses and optional refundable deposits within the founder’s approved advertising budget.

Days 12–14 Indicative Target

5. Review the evidence and consider a Go, Revise or Stop recommendation

Review acquisition costs, the score range and supplier order risk. Go leads to a supplier brief and staged spending plan. Revise identifies what to change and retest. Stop explains why the evidence does not support committing to the order.

Indicative pilot schedule note: Timings (such as 14 days) and sprint budgets represent indicative pilot targets subject to development and testing, rather than guaranteed results. All testing is conducted under founder direction.
Structured Outcomes

The Go, Revise or Stop Decision Framework

LaunchProof’s planned decision framework will combine multiple factors to assess product viability and support an unambiguous recommendation. The founder retains complete decision authority.

Go

Proceed to stock order

Customer evidence confirms willingness to pay at a price that leaves a viable margin, and projected demand justifies the supplier’s minimum order. Go leads to a supplier brief and staged spending plan.

Revise

Refine and retest

Customers respond well to the concept or price, but acquisition costs or the supplier’s minimum order quantity create excess initial risk. Revise identifies what to change and retest before ordering stock.

Stop

Preserve capital

Customer demand or unit economics fail to support placing an order under realistic marketing costs. Stop explains why the evidence does not support committing to stock, preserving capital for other opportunities.
